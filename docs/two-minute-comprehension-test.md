# Two-minute comprehension test

Recruit at least five people unfamiliar with the project. Include visitors using a phone, keyboard navigation, and reduced motion where practical. Do not explain the science before they start.

Ask each person to open Zentrix and explore freely for two minutes. Observe without coaching; note hesitations, missed controls, the time to the first interaction, and whether the participant reaches the sensor, the neighbor explanation, and the black-and-white experiment. The suggested milestones are targets, not deadlines: understand the premise by 15 seconds, try the sensor by 40 seconds, see neighbors by 70 seconds, and explore false color by 100 seconds.

After two minutes, ask in their own words:

1. Does each sensor spot measure every color component?
2. Where do the missing components come from?
3. Why can the black-and-white pattern become colored?
4. What changed when you moved the sensor?
5. What is one thing you learned about cameras?

Record answers and observations without prompting. A target is for at least four of five unfamiliar participants to explain the mechanism correctly; this target is not evidence of success until the test is conducted. Note whether they understand that the photo experiment is a simulation and that reducing false color can also reduce detail. Record accessibility, mobile-layout, and WebGL-fallback issues separately.
